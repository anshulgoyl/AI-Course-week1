Housing pressure across Europe

The European average fell from 11.5% in 2014 to 7.7% in 2025. Yet this broad improvement contains sharply different national experiences: some countries have steadily unwound pressure, while others have recently moved upward.

Strategic typology

Four different affordability stories

These patterns describe the shape of housing pressure: its level today, its trajectory since 2014, and whether the recent picture looks settled or unsettled. They are descriptive groupings, not explanations of cause.

Persistent pressure, partial recovery

High levelFalling long run

Greece · Norway

The national rate is far below its earlier peak, but still sits at the high end of Europe. This is a recovery story with a persistent affordability burden rather than a return to a low-pressure baseline.

Low pressure, suddenly exposed

Sharp recent moveMonitor now

Denmark · Sweden · Estonia

The national series has turned upward after a comparatively lower-pressure period. The key feature is volatility: the latest reading looks less like a settled level and more like a recent change in the housing-cost environment.

Structural improvement

Large 2014–25 fallLower 2025 level

Romania · Poland · Bulgaria · Croatia

The burden is materially below its 2014 level. These countries share a broad pattern of improvement, although the settlement profile can still reveal pockets where city, suburban or rural households experience a different reality.

Stable, low-pressure baseline

Low levelSmall movement

Finland · Slovenia · Cyprus · Malta

The headline rate is low and relatively steady. That tells us the burden is not broad-based nationally, while the urbanisation view helps show whether the remaining pressure is concentrated in particular types of place.
